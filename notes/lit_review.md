# Literature review — entity resolution of person records at scale

*Prepared for the Person-ER Embedding Lab. Method: a 13-agent structured literature sweep (8 core
areas, a completeness critic, 4 follow-up areas the critic demanded), run August 2026, with an
explicit epistemic rule: only highly established, reality-tested methods are recorded as settled;
every published benchmark number, leaderboard claim, and author's framing of their own contribution
is recorded as a hypothesis to test. Citations below are to sources the sweep verified; where a host
was unreachable from the research sandbox and a fact rests on search-index snippets, the section says
so.*

## 0. Positioning statement

**What is settled.** Classical record linkage is a mature engineering discipline with a stable core:
the Fellegi–Sunter model with EM-estimated match/unmatch probabilities, term-frequency adjustment,
binned string comparators (Jaro–Winkler and relatives), and multi-pass OR-combined blocking. Every
national-scale production system we could find — census bureaus, national health services, statistics
offices — is a *hybrid*: a deterministic cascade first, probabilistic scoring on the residual,
clerical review in the gray zone. Two replicated empirical facts anchor the lab's framing: linkage
error is *demographically differential* (missed matches concentrate in minority, deprived, and
high-mobility populations — replicated across English hospital data, census linkage, and patient
matching), and transitive closure over pairwise decisions produces *chain-merge pathologies* at scale
(mega-components glued together by hub values and shared households). Meanwhile, the deep-ER
literature of 2018–2026 is real progress on the wrong substrate for our question: overwhelmingly
product/bibliographic benchmarks, pairwise F1 on fixed candidate sets, records ≤1e6, single-run
results without variance.

**What is open.** The sweep found, with striking convergence across independently-run areas, that the
lab's exact setting is nearly unoccupied: there is no public, statistically sound, entity-level
evaluation of whole-record learned embeddings against a *well-tuned* Fellegi–Sunter baseline on
person records — at any scale, let alone with noise-swept multi-seed comparisons, measured noise
prevalence, group-correlated noise exposure, leakage-controlled entity-disjoint splits, validated
scaling extrapolation, or causal "training pressure → embedding property → system metric" chains.
Each ingredient is individually documented as missing in the sections below; the combination is this
lab's territory.

**Where this lab can say something new.** Our novelty claims are deliberately *protocol and
measurement* claims, not algorithm claims. We renounce: novel losses, miners, index structures,
clustering algorithms, parsers, and any live 1e9 benchmark. We claim, and the sections below gate
each claim against the already-explored design space: (1) a Musgrave-style budget-matched,
CI-reported comparison of embedding training pressures for person ER; (2) a corruption model
*calibrated by measurement* (same-person field diffs across real snapshots) with per-group exposure —
which no existing generator expresses; (3) a ranking-invariance-across-noise-models experiment; (4) a
coverage-validated bootstrap protocol for ER metrics; (5) an operating-point protocol demonstration
(fixed-precision / cost-explicit, never per-method best-F1); (6) a CheckList-style invariance battery — delivered as methodology (the notebook-08 battery, exercised by TRN-03/TRN-06's slice scoring) rather than a standalone matrix experiment —
for record embeddings with SHOULD-hold and MUST-NOT-hold slices; (7) mediation-checked
pressure→property→metric chains; (8) an administrative-key circularity treatment (audit the truth key
before trusting it); (9) out-of-sample-validated scaling curves plus EVT tail analysis for
false-positive mass at 10–100× scale; (10) measurement of false-negative contamination in hard-negative
mining for dedup-dense corpora; (11) entity- and household-disjoint split protocols for person data
with measured leakage optimism; (12) a matched-budget adjudication of the contested sparse-vs-dense
blocking question on noisy person records.

**Scope note — privacy is intentionally out of scope.** This lab studies matching quality,
statistics, and scale on public-record and synthetic data. Privacy-preserving record linkage,
embedding-inversion and membership-inference risk, DP training, and the legal status of person
embeddings are deliberately excluded from the lab's experiments and claims. Section 10 below surveys
that landscape for one purpose only: to mark the boundary and inform basic data-handling hygiene
(the repo ships download scripts, never data).

**Reading rails.** Four biases the sweep's critic documented, which this review and the lab's writing
must not inherit: (i) *single-source amplification* — Splink's performance and recipe claims trace to
its maintainers, and the closest industrial precedent for our hypothesis (a 200M-record hybrid
embedding+rules patient-matching system) is one unreplicated vendor paper; both are hypotheses we
test, not evidence we cite. (ii) *Operating-point incoherence* — most published cross-system deltas
compare systems at different, per-method-optimized thresholds; we treat all such numbers as existence
claims, never effect sizes. (iii) *Noise-model unrealism* — the flagship census-scale simulation
testbed applies column noise independently of attributes and columns, so it structurally cannot
express the group-correlated noise exposure that the disparity literature identifies as the dominant
mechanism; realism claims must be earned by measurement. (iv) *Truth-key circularity* — the standard
person-ER benchmarks derive ground truth from administrative keys whose own error is unaudited, and
demographic labels used for disparity measurement are themselves noisy; every claim built on either
carries the correction machinery or the caveat.

## 1. Classical record linkage at scale

### What is settled

Fellegi-Sunter (FS) is still the formal backbone of probabilistic person linkage: per-field agreement patterns, m/u probabilities, log-likelihood match weights, and two thresholds bracketing a clerical-review gray zone; it is the core of Splink, fastLink, Match*Pro, and most NSO and health pipelines (Fellegi & Sunter 1969; Winkler RR93-8, per sweep sources). EM estimation of m/u without labels (Winkler 1988; Jaro 1989, per sweep sources) is standard and works in favorable regimes, but conditional independence is routinely violated in person data (name-ethnicity correlations, household effects), and ignoring the dependence measurably biases weights — replicated over decades. Term-frequency adjustment of name-value weights is settled directional practice, not a paper claim, and Jaro-Winkler is the de facto short-name comparator, with the settled second-order finding that no comparator dominates, so production systems bin scores into agreement levels and mix JW with q-gram and phonetic variants (Christen 2006 TR-CS-06-02, per sweep sources).

At 1e8+, blocking is mandatory and settled practice is multi-pass disjunctive keys: Census BigMatch ran ~10 simultaneous passes over a billion-record file (institutional report), ONS used 29 hierarchical matchkeys for Census 2021, and Splink OR-combines blocking rules (per sweep sources). Production pipelines are hybrid — deterministic cascade first, FS on the residual, clerical gray zone — documented at Census PVS, NHS Master Person Service (~60M persons), ONS, ABS, and Stats NZ IDI (per sweep sources). The deterministic-vs-probabilistic trade-off has a near decision rule: at roughly <=5% missingness/error per linkage field (simulation-derived boundary, Zhu et al. 2015, per sweep sources), deterministic matchkeys match probabilistic quality at lower cost; beyond that, probabilistic linkage recovers matches that are demographically non-random. Differential linkage error is replicated fact: HES deterministic missed-match rates fell from 8.6% (1998) to 0.4% (2015) (observational, replicated across HES studies), with misses concentrated among ethnic minorities, deprived areas, and migrants, biasing downstream analyses (Hagger-Johnson/Harron line; Lancet Digital Health 2021, per sweep sources).

Splink is the reference open implementation, deployed by multiple institutions (MoJ, ONS, ABS 2025 Spine, NHS England — institutionally reported); its performance and scale figures are maintainer-sourced and therefore live in the hypotheses paragraph below, not here on DuckDB (Splink docs, per sweep sources). dedupe is memory-bound well below 1e8; JedAI/pyJedAI are research workbenches, not NSO engines. Entities come from thresholded transitive closure, and threshold choice materially reshapes clusters (Splink docs; Binette & Steorts, per sweep sources). The standard academic EM benchmarks contain almost no person data at scale and are largely saturated, so deep-ER wins there are not evidence for person ER at 1e8+ (Papadakis et al. arXiv 2307.01231; Binette & Steorts 2022, per sweep sources).

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

Splink's performance and deployed-scale figures — ~1M-record dedup per minute on a laptop, 7M records / ~1e9 comparisons in ~2 minutes, 100M+ records linked at MoJ — are maintainer-reported and independently unbenchmarked; BAS-01 measures them ourselves. The head-to-head tool numbers are single-study: on simulated pseudopeople Rhode Island data, Splink ~579k TP / 3 FP vs fastLink ~582k TP / 137 FP without SSN, and ~12x faster (NAACCR 2024 / IJPDS 2786, per sweep sources) — one run, one noise configuration, thresholds wildly different across tools (0.995 vs 1-1e-9): confounded by operating point. Referential matching strongly outperformed conventional probabilistic matching on a single HIE's 30k-pair gold standard (Grannis et al. JAMIA 2022, per sweep sources; referential sensitivity 0.935 at PPV ~0.9995 — note the sweep records the probabilistic 0.637 figure inconsistently, as sensitivity in this area and as F-score in §9's area, a citation conflict its own critic flagged: verify against the paper before citing either reading). Truveta's claim that contrastive PII embeddings gated by deterministic rules outperform baselines in production over 200M+ patients is a single vendor paper, unreplicated — the closest existing system to this lab's working hypothesis. The 2026 finding that name embeddings cut ethnic missed-match disparities but raise false matches in high-corruption groups (arXiv 2601.07693, per sweep sources) is one paper; so is EnsembleLink (arXiv 2601.21138). fuzzylink's result that embedding similarity plus LLM labels beats fastLink on messy name-only tasks carries its authors' own boundary hypothesis — FS likely wins when many structured fields exist (Ornstein 2026, per sweep sources) — and both halves are untested on person demographics. ONS's MinHash-LSH blocking result (100% pairs recall at ~8x fewer candidates, IJPDS 2318) is a small parameter-sensitive exploration, not production evidence. Institutional self-assessments — ABS's Splink-vs-deterministic parity claim, ONS's <0.01% automatic-matching FP target (stated target, not verified attainment), PVS's ~2pp match-rate edge over SSA's EVS — are reported, not independently verified. EM's identifiability conditions (Winkler-era) and Splink's hybrid direct+EM training recipe are institutional experience and maintainer claim respectively. Vendor figures that deterministic MPI matching misses "20-40%" of true matches are marketing-adjacent and unverified.

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

Already tried: dependence-aware log-linear FS extensions, Bayesian FS with uncertainty propagation, fastLink's missing-data EM; the full comparator families plus supervised combinations over similarity vectors; blocking from standard keys through sorted neighborhood, canopies, q-grams, LSH, and meta-blocking (surveyed, but mostly on non-person data); pipeline archetypes (spine-based vs pairwise, 1:1 constraints, cascade-then-probabilistic); tool head-to-heads on simulated registry data; and embedding hybrids around the FS core (fuzzylink, EnsembleLink, Truveta, fairness-oriented name embeddings, ONS LSH). Re-confirmation, not novelty: TF adjustment helping names, JW strength on names, multi-pass blocking beating single keys, probabilistic beating deterministic under heavy noise, another single-run pairwise-metric tool comparison. What is new is the matched-operating-point, multi-seed, entity-level version of any of these.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

No public, statistically sound, entity-level benchmark at 1e8+ exists; pseudopeople makes a census-scale one feasible (subject to its independent-column noise limitation, §0(iii)/§12); nobody has published one — this is the lab's core lane, run on NC data plus the lab's own calibrated generator with pseudopeople as an audited substrate (PLAN §4) (MET-02 operating points, MET-03 bootstrap units, MET-04 variance/power, MET-01 rank reversals, feasibility-gated by MET-05, with extrapolation via SCL-01). The NAACCR threshold-comparability mess maps directly to MET-02. Where embeddings earn their place against a well-tuned FS baseline is exactly BAS-01 plus the HYB-01 noise-regime map, with NSE-01/NSE-03 supplying measured noise and ranking-invariance checks. Blocking recall at scale, asserted but unmeasured, maps to BAS-02. Component-wise fairness attribution maps to FAIR-01 under NSE-01 exposure measurement, with TRN-06 probing the nickname/twin channel. Pairwise-to-cluster error propagation maps to CLU-01 with MET-01. The NSO reproducibility gap motivates NSE-02 (generator fidelity) and MET-06 (identifiability accounting). Honestly unmapped: systematic EM-identifiability mapping (BAS-01 touches it incidentally), clerical-review economics, and constraint handling (1:1 assignment, household structure) at scale.

## 2. Modern deep-learning ER

### What is settled

The architectural skeleton is not in dispute: systems decompose into cheap candidate generation plus an expensive pairwise decision, and retrieve-and-rerank (bi-encoder retrieval, cross-encoder/LLM rerank) is the same pattern, production-proven in adjacent entity-linking/IR work (BLINK-style, per sweep sources). Ditto-style transformer cross-encoders outperform pre-transformer matchers (DeepER, DeepMatcher) with less labeled data and better noise robustness — replicated across many independent groups (per sweep sources).

Equally settled and more consequential here: the canonical deep-ER evidence base contains essentially no person records. Magellan/DeepMatcher, WDC Products, and Machamp are products, bibliography, restaurants, music, and companies; person-record evidence lives in separate literatures (probabilistic linkage, patient MPI, census linking, PPRL) that deep-ER papers rarely cite, so any claimed transfer of benchmark numbers to person records is unsupported.

Learned dense blocking has not shown a consistent advantage over well-tuned sparse blocking: Sparkly's top-k TF/IDF beat 8 state-of-the-art blockers with recall 86.57–99.96% (one group, convergent with ShallowBlocker 2024 and Zeakis et al. VLDB 2023's mixed results and vectorization overheads); multiple groups document deep blockers as unstable and tuning-sensitive.

The evaluation critique is settled. Pairwise F1 on small balanced candidate-pair benchmarks is a biased, unstable estimator: quadratic error opportunity makes naive pairwise precision over-optimistic and can flip rankings (Binette & Steorts; PatentsView); test sets under roughly 150 matches give unstable F1 (noted independently by Mannheim and third parties); construction artifacts — balanced labels, within-candidate-set pairs, train/test entity sharing — inflate scores (IJCAI 2022; WDC Products). Fine-tuned matchers degrade on unseen entities and datasets — settled as a phenomenon; the reported 36–56 F1-point cross-dataset drop varies by setup.

On the person side, Fellegi–Sunter with blocking is production-deployed at the lab's scale (Splink — institutionally reported at 1e8; the scale figures themselves are maintainer-sourced existence claims, §1) — the explainable baseline any embedding must beat. Census linking traces a replicated FP/FN frontier where algorithm choice changes downstream conclusions; phonetic-only false-match rates are reported at 20–70% (direction replicated; magnitudes contested). Batch composition and negative selection materially change bi-encoder quality: R-SupCon loses 55 and 37 F1 points without source-aware sampling (one group's numbers; direction replicated in Block-SCL, SC-Block, Sudowoodo, and IR). ANN index machinery at 1e8–1e9 vectors is mature for in-distribution image/text workloads — but the only true 1e9 leaderboard dates to 2021 and OOD recall collapses (§6), so for noisy short-field person embeddings, whether search is a solved sub-problem is itself what BAS-02/SCL-02 test. LLM API matchers cost orders of magnitude more per pair (gap settled; exact ratios single-paper).

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

System ingredients: Ditto's +9.8 F1 over DeepMatcher with per-ingredient contributions (self-reported ablation); HierGAT beating Ditto (single group); Sudowoodo's up-to-+16% F1 with a third fewer labels (self-reported); Paganelli's claim that EM fine-tuning mainly alters last layers and exploits token overlap (one group).

LLM matchers: GPT-4-class zero-shot beating transferred PLMs by 40–68 F1 (single group; test sets downsampled to 1,250 pairs; contamination flagged by the authors); fine-tuned small models roughly matching prompted large LLMs cross-dataset (single group); AnyMatch within 4.4% of MatchGPT at a claimed 3,899x lower per-token cost (one workshop paper); BATCHER's 4–7x cost savings, ComEM's select-from-candidates advantage, and LLM-CER's quality-per-dollar win (each single-group). Benchmark contamination in LLM pretraining: suspected, unquantified.

Blocking: DeepBlocker's autoencoder-best finding (single group, partially contradicted by Sparkly/ShallowBlocker); UniBlocker's ~+10 mAP (single paper); SC-Block's amortized-runtime argument (single group).

Person records: Truveta's hybrid siamese-BERT-plus-rules system on 200M+ PII records (industrial, private, unreplicated — the closest published test of the lab's exact hypothesis); LinkTransformer's "wide margin" over string matching (self-reported, heterogeneous tasks); multi-agent RAG's 94.3% accuracy on synthetic 200–300-record segments (very weak); Amazon's lessons on bake-offs, rule vetoes, and transitive-merge re-verification (industrial single source); WDC's claim that unseen-entity drops reorder systems and hit contrastive matchers hardest (needs person replication); Binette-style entity-centric estimators as approximately unbiased (principled, thinly validated). The central negative is itself a hypothesis: no public study shows whole-record embeddings beating tuned Fellegi–Sunter on the same large person dataset.

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

Pair classifiers are thoroughly mined: attribute-aligned RNN/attention (DeepER, DeepMatcher's taxonomy), PLM cross-encoders (Ditto, JointBERT), graph interdependence (HierGAT, GNEM), explainability layers. Low-resource pressures on product data: transfer plus active learning, augmentation (Ditto, Rotom), self-supervision (Sudowoodo), supervised contrastive with source-aware sampling (R-SupCon), blocking-derived hard negatives (Block-SCL), generalized formulations (Machamp, PromptEM), unified multi-task models (Unicorn, Jellyfish). The 2023–2026 LLM space is heavily mined: prompting, example selection, batch prompting, interaction granularity, fine-tuning, distillation, cascades, in-context clustering, agentic proposals. Blocking spans rules, LSH, set-similarity joins, TF/IDF top-k, dense variants, and off-the-shelf-embedding benchmarks; pipelines include pyJedAI, FAMER's clustering with repair, TransClean, and merge re-verification; evaluation design covers difficulty dimensions, open-environment reconstruction, cross-dataset protocols, and entity-centric estimation.

Another matcher/blocker bake-off on product benchmarks, another prompt study, or re-demonstrating unseen-entity degradation on WDC is re-confirmation. Unexplored: systematic training-pressure ablations for whole-record person embeddings; unseen-entity splits for person data; entity-level metrics as the training target; public 1e8+ person benchmarks; embedding-vs-Fellegi–Sunter under matched compute; multi-seed statistical protocols.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

The person-record evidence gap (Truveta is the only at-scale publication, unreplicated) is the lab's whole program: noise realism to NSE-01/NSE-02, training to the TRN family. No public 1e8–1e9 person benchmark, plus dated Febrl/GeCo noise models never validated against real PII errors: NSE-01/02/03, plus SCL-01 for validated extrapolation past the 1e7 live ceiling. Near-total absence of entity-level evaluation (pairwise-F1-to-cluster-quality mapping unmeasured; one false link merges hundreds of records): MET-01, MET-03, MET-05, CLU-01. Missing statistical soundness (no multi-seed variance or CIs; orderings like HierGAT>Ditto may not survive proper treatment): MET-02, MET-04, MET-07. Scarce causal training-pressure studies: TRN-01–TRN-06. Dense-blocking recall beyond 1e7: BAS-02 at matched budget, SCL-01/02/03 for the billion-scale frontier, BAS-01 as the tuned incumbent. When rules earn their place (descriptive treatments only): HYB-01. Unseen-entity generalization for persons: MET-07 and X02. The bi-encoder-vs-cross-encoder frontier for persons: X01 (EXPL). LLM benchmark contamination is left unmapped — the lab runs no LLM matchers. Reconciling census-linking evaluation discipline with deep-ER practice: MET-05's audited error accounting and MET-01.

## 3. Collective ER and clustering: from pairs to entities

### What is settled

An explicit pairs-to-entities clustering stage is necessary, not optional: pairwise classifiers generically produce intransitive decisions, so every serious pipeline — research surveys, Splink, FAMER, Intelius, JedAI — inserts graph clustering after scoring (Christophides et al. CSUR 2021; Splink docs, per sweep sources). Transitive closure / connected components is the maximum-recall, minimum-cost choice and is precision-fragile: single false-positive bridge edges chain-merge distinct entities. This is replicated in independent benchmarks (Hassanzadeh et al. VLDB 2009; Saeedi et al. 2017, where CC had the worst F-measure of all schemes) and production (Intelius/CCF found giant components of 53M, 25M, and 17M records in a 6B-record person graph — production evidence, proprietary detail). Precision-oriented families (center, merge-center, star, pivot-style correlation clustering) trade recall for precision, and no clustering scheme dominates across datasets and thresholds — replicated in at least three independent studies (Hassanzadeh 2009; Saeedi 2017/2018; Draisbach et al. 2019, per sweep sources).

Correlation clustering theory is settled at the theorem level: minimizing disagreements is NP-hard (Bansal-Blum-Chawla), random-pivot KwikCluster is an expected 3-approximation, and parallel variants achieve near-linear speedups with replicated implementations (Pan et al. 2015; Cohen-Addad et al. 2021, per sweep sources). Connected components at 1e10-1e12+ edges is solved engineering: CCF computed 435M components from 6B nodes / 92B edges in ~7 hours (production report); Google, Yahoo (trillions of edges), and Splink's SQL-backend CC at 1e8 person scale (maintainer-reported) is consistent with O(log n)-round designs (per sweep sources). Relational/co-occurrence evidence (co-authorship, households, families) improves ER accuracy over attribute-only matching in relationally rich domains — the direction is replicated over 20 years (Bhattacharya-Getoor 2007; Dong et al. 2005; Kouki et al. 2017/2019; RELATER 2023); magnitudes and scalability are not settled. On measurement: pairwise and cluster-level metrics can rank systems differently, and pairwise F1 is dominated by the largest clusters because pair counts grow quadratically in cluster size — replicated (Menestrina et al. 2010; Binette et al. 2024; PatentsView 2022). Person-entity clusters are microclusters: sizes grow sublinearly in corpus size, so balanced-cluster assumptions are wrong for person ER (Bayesian ER literature; consistent with Intelius's mean component size of ~14, a production statistic). Production systems at 1e8+ with simple clustering plus safeguards exist (Splink/ONS/NHS, Senzing, Intelius); their existence is fact, their relative quality is not.

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

The clustering-scheme league tables are single-group results at modest scale: CC worst-F with CCPivot/Center/Star-2 more threshold-robust, and CLIP's source-consistency clustering beating and repairing other schemes, both come from the Leipzig FAMER line on <=10M NCVR records with synthetic GeCo corruption (Saeedi et al. 2017/2018, per sweep sources); Draisbach et al. 2019's repair-beats-TC and Hassanzadeh 2009's Ricochet threshold-insensitivity are likewise unreplicated on other person corpora. The plausible reading that the clustering-choice gap shrinks as the similarity graph gets cleaner is implied by FAMER experiments but was never isolated as a controlled finding. Scalable-HAC numbers are single-group on proprietary infrastructure: TeraHAC's (1+epsilon)-approximate HAC on 8-trillion-edge graphs, ~100x fewer rounds, 8.3x faster than SCC at 1.16x quality (Dhulipala et al. SIGMOD 2024); SCC's 30B-point runs (Monath et al. KDD 2021). Correlation-clustering approximation ratios are evolving — the cluster-LP line claimed 1.437 but the proof had a gap, ~1.485 stands (STOC 2024, per sweep sources) — and "practical heuristics match LP quality" is a claim, not a theorem. Collective and incremental results are dataset-bound: PSL familial models beating pairwise-with-relational-features (Kouki et al., NIH/Wikidata data); incremental merge/split repair matching batch quality orders of magnitude faster (Gruenheid et al. VLDB 2014); GNN over-merge detection (Amazon, workshop paper, product clusters); graph-metric plus LLM-active-learning repair (JDIQ 2025); label-free FP pruning via transitive consistency (TransClean 2025). The 2025-2026 LLM-era claims — in-context set clustering up to +10% F at 5x fewer calls (SIGMOD 2025), Alper's label-propagation dominance on 8 dirty-ER benchmarks (KDD 2026), and the industry lesson that re-verifying every cross-cluster merge blocks chain-merges (Pavani et al. 2026, 864-5M record benchmarks) — are new, single-paper, and not person-scale.

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

Already benchmarked or built: the full unconstrained clustering zoo (TC, Center, Merge-Center, Star, Ricochet, MinCut, Markov, correlation-clustering variants, HAC); constrained clustering (CLIP source-consistency, 8 bipartite one-to-one algorithms over 700 similarity graphs); scalable hierarchical clustering (affinity clustering, ParHAC, SCC, TeraHAC — open-sourced in google/graph-mining); distributed CC engineering end to end; collective inference machinery from relational agglomerative clustering through MLN/PSL, Dedupalog, message-passing scaling, GNN cluster QA, and LLM-guided label propagation; cluster repair as a distinct stage; incremental/streaming frameworks; entity-level evaluation methodology; and household/temporal person-graph linkage (RELATER). Re-confirmation, not novelty: another <=10M-record clustering bake-off on NCVR-plus-GeCo with point-estimate pairwise F1, demonstrating that TC is precision-fragile, or that relational signals beat weak attribute-only baselines. Explicitly not yet done, per the sweep: whole-record person embeddings as the similarity substrate for large-scale clustering comparisons.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

No controlled study at 1e8-1e9 person scale isolates clustering-scheme choice from pairwise-score quality with entity-level metrics and statistical significance — this is CLU-01's core question, run under MET-03/MET-04 machinery and extrapolated via SCL-01. The embedding-clustering interaction (does calibrated embedding similarity re-rank clustering schemes; does embedding geometry create distinctive chain-merge modes via hubness of frequent-name vectors) maps to CLU-01 plus SCL-02. The absence of a noise model that reproduces production chain-merge pathologies (hub values, shared addresses, default DOBs) maps to NSE-01/NSE-02. Joint tuning of threshold, calibration, and clustering objective — including exploiting correlation clustering's implicit log-odds weighting — maps to CAL-01 with MET-02 and CLU-01. Entity-level statistically sound comparison methodology maps to MET-01/MET-03/MET-04. Entity-ID stability and churn under incremental clustering maps to X02 (exploratory). Whether graph metrics (bridges, density, centralization) actually predict cluster error is folklore the lab can test only within CLU-01's scope, not as a dedicated study. Honestly unmapped: scalable collective inference (household signals on top of a strong learned pairwise scorer), constrained clustering at trillion-edge scale, GDPR-style deletion/retroactive correction, and person-scale LLM-guided collective ER (X01's reranker touches budgeted strong-model queries only tangentially).

## 4. Evaluation practice and statistics

### What is settled

Metric choice in ER is a substantive modeling decision, not a formality: different metrics rank the same ER outputs differently, shown by Menestrina, Whang & Garcia-Molina (VLDB 2010, per sweep sources) and replicated in author-name disambiguation (Kim, Scientometrics 2019) and coreference (Moosavi & Strube, ACL 2016). Their Generalized Merge Distance subsumes pairwise and several cluster measures as special cases of a merge/split cost function, in linear time. Hand & Christen (Statistics and Computing 2018) proved the F-measure is a weighted mean of precision and recall whose implicit weight depends on the evaluated method's own output — so comparing methods at per-method best-F1 applies a different tradeoff to each. The standard cluster-metric families (B-cubed, CEAF, MUC, cluster-F, K-metric, splitting/lumping) have settled definitions, and Variation of Information is a true metric on partitions (Meila 2003); which family to prefer is not settled.

Because non-match pairs grow quadratically in n, pairwise accuracy/ROC-style metrics are uninformative, and blocking is evaluated with the RR/PC/PQ triad, with known limitations (RR quality-blind, PQ ignores redundant comparisons; Papadakis et al., ACM CSUR 2020). Naive pairwise metrics on non-representative labeled subsets are biased estimates of full-data performance; valid estimation requires design-based estimators matched to the sampling scheme (Binette & Steorts 2023; Binette et al. 2024). The DeepMatcher/Magellan-family benchmarks use pair-disjoint but not record- or entity-disjoint splits, and matchers evaluated on entity-disjoint splits perform qualitatively worse — the construction fact is verifiable, the gap replicated (Peeters & Bizer's WDC Products; Wang et al., IJCAI 2022). Small-data transformer fine-tuning has high seed variance (Mosbach et al., ICLR 2021), so single-run single-split F1 comparisons on small test sets are unreliable in principle. Linkage error is non-random — it concentrates on records with poor identifiers and on disadvantaged groups, and propagates downstream (Harron et al., IJE 2017; GUILD; Census PVS assessments). The settled national-scale playbook: clerical review of probabilistic samples, gold-identifier truth decks, model-based false-match-rate estimation, downstream sensitivity analysis, transparent reporting (Harron/GUILD; Census CARRA WP 2014-02; Splink evaluation docs). Finally, some records are unresolvable in principle — Johndrow, Lum & Dunson (Biometrika 2018) prove accurate ER is impossible unless entities are few relative to records or well separated — and Bayesian ER (dblink, JCGS 2021) demonstrates posterior uncertainty over linkage structure as an established alternative to point estimates.

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

All of the following are single-group or single-dataset claims. Naive within-benchmark-cluster precision sits near 1 while design-based estimators reveal different rankings — shown on 400 PatentsView inventor clusters only (Binette & Steorts 2023). OASIS achieves asymptotically optimal-variance P/R/F estimates with orders-of-magnitude fewer labels (Marchant & Rubinstein, VLDB 2017) — unreplicated, untested at 1e8–1e9. TRL-Bench (2026) measured >50% test-side record overlap with train+valid in 11/16 DeepMatcher-family sources (Abt-Buy 94.2%/95.0%) — directly verifiable, but one group's numbers until recomputed. All matchers dropping significantly on WDC Products' unseen-entity splits is products-only evidence (Peeters & Bizer). Six of 13 classic benchmarks having linearity >0.8 rests on one probe suite (Papadakis et al. 2023); the open-environment performance collapse is one group's reconstruction (Wang et al., IJCAI 2022). Kim's usage census (pairwise-F in 15/23 AND studies) and the B-cubed≡K-metric precision/recall identity are one author's survey plus an algebraically checkable claim. LEA's fix for the mention-identification confound (Moosavi & Strube) has untested transfer to dirty person records. The Syrian-conflict unique-entity estimate (191,874 vs 191,369 manual, from ~450K of 63B pairs; Chen, Shrivastava & Steorts, AOAS 2018) is a single flagship application. Smaller fine-tuned LMs showing higher cross-seed variance is one study (EDBT 2025). Jackknife beating bootstrap for pairwise-agreement CIs (Severiano et al., PLOS ONE 2011) comes from outside ER. Unsupervised graph-structure evaluation (JDIQ 2025) and the Case Count Metric System (Talburt et al., 2026) are untested outside their authors' groups. Belin-Rubin FMR accuracy and non-random-PIK selection bias are single-agency Census estimates (CARRA WP 2014-02; CES-WP-26-06). F* = F/(2−F) is an interpretability proposal, not an empirical result (Hand, Christen & Kirielle 2021). McNemar's test on discordant pairs has been used for matcher comparison, but its validity under ER's dependent pairs is exactly what is untested.

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

The metric zoo is thoroughly charted (pairwise P/R/F1, cluster-F, B-cubed, CEAF, MUC, K-metric, splitting/lumping, Rand/ARI, VI, GMD, LEA, BLANC, Talburt-Wang, CCMS, F*), as are comparative metric-disagreement studies in ER, AND, and coreference. Blocking evaluation (RR/PC/PQ, candidate-recall@budget), sampling-based evaluation (stratified passive, OASIS, cluster-sampling estimators, LSH-adaptive entity counting, clerical review, Belin-Rubin FMR, unsupervised estimation), split design (pair-level random, fixed, WDC's 27-variant multi-dimensional design, open-environment reconstruction, difficulty profiling, overlap audits, ICE-ID), and statistical machinery outside EM papers (McNemar, bootstrap/jackknife, multi-seed norms, Bayesian posteriors) are all explored. Re-demonstrating that metrics disagree, that best-F1 comparisons are flawed, or that classic benchmarks leak would be re-confirmation, not novelty; the unexplored remainder is in the gaps below.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

The absence of any coverage-validated resampling protocol (pair vs record vs entity vs block as bootstrap unit) is the lab's MET-03; metric rank-reversal on our own outputs is MET-01; the unresolved post-Hand-Christen operating-point question is MET-02 with CAL-01 supplying calibrated thresholds. Variance decomposition (seed/split/benchmark-sampling/annotation) and power analysis is MET-04. The lack of a leakage-controlled, entity-disjoint person benchmark at scale is engaged by MET-07 (split-optimism quantification) with NSE-02 supplying noise-model fidelity. Incomplete-ground-truth bias in RR/PC/PQ and proxy-label error are engaged via MET-05 (NCID error audit, a feasibility gate) and, for demographically correlated proxy-label availability, FAIR-01. Identifiability accounting — reporting the unresolvable fraction and conditioning metrics on resolvable subsets — is MET-06 (with SCL-04's analytical ambiguity budget). Entity-level production monitoring connects partially to X02 (entity-ID churn, exploratory). Honestly unmapped: independent replication of OASIS/design-based estimators, scalable 1e9 cluster-metric computation with CIs, LLM-era benchmark contamination measurement, and calibration of ground-truth-free evaluators (CCMS, JDIQ 2025) against design-based estimates.

## 5. Scale-dependence of error and extrapolation methodology

Sweep-level caveat: this area was compiled without web access, from model knowledge through January 2026; every URL and figure is from memory and must be re-verified before citation.

### What is settled

The core mechanical fact is comparison-space arithmetic: with bounded duplicate-cluster sizes, non-match pair mass grows ~n²/2 while true-match mass grows ~linearly, so a scorer with a fixed per-pair false-match rate u accumulates ~u·n²/2 expected false positives and pairwise precision → 0 as n grows; holding false positives per record constant requires per-pair FMR ~1/n — implicit in Fellegi & Sunter (1969), explicit in Christen & Goiser (2007). The identification counterpart FPIR(N) = 1−(1−FMR)^N ≈ N·FMR is codified in ISO/IEC 19795-style evaluation; FRVT-documented deviations from linearity make the union bound a first-order model, not an exact law. EVT machinery is settled mathematics, and for Gaussian-like non-match tails the expected maximum of N scores grows ~sqrt(2 ln N) — a fixed threshold mechanically loses precision with corpus size, a moving threshold loses recall. Empirical gallery-size degradation is replicated (MegaFace distractor sweeps to 1e6; NIST FRVT to ~1e7 identities); functional forms are model-dependent.

Surname distributions are heavy-tailed everywhere studied, with country-dependent concentration (2010 US Census: ~6.3M distinct surnames among ~295M people, top names ~0.8% each — figures from memory per the sweep caveat). Pairwise field-collision probability is the Renyi-2 mass Σp², long computed for surnames as random isonymy (Lasker tradition); Eckersley (2010) supplies surprisal accounting. DOB heaping and the age pyramid raise DOB collision mass above uniform. The US twin rate has been ~31–34 per 1,000 births since the 2010s (CDC NVSS); twins and same-address Jr/Sr pairs are the canonical hardest false-match classes (Winkler) — a nonzero irreducible-ambiguity floor for demographic-only person ER. Fellegi-Sunter u-probabilities are population-frequency-dependent, re-estimated per corpus with term-frequency adjustments (Winkler; Splink; fastLink). Blocking keys inherit Zipfian skew: the largest block's pair count grows quadratically absent purging/capping (Papadakis et al., CSUR 2020). Per-record heterogeneity of false-match propensity is replicated — biometric menagerie (Doddington; Yager & Dunstone), hubness (Radovanovic et al.) — so extrapolating from a global mean per-pair FPR is structurally wrong. Existence proofs: Aadhaar deduplicates ~1.3–1.4B enrollments 1:N with multimodal biometrics, having explicitly rejected demographic-only dedup at that scale; Daugman's ~200 billion iris cross-comparisons validated a binomial impostor-tail model at per-pair FMR ~1e-10 to 1e-11, the regime 1e9-scale dedup requires; Census PVS runs FS-style demographic linkage against a ~3e8 reference file. Percolation is settled random-graph mathematics — cross-cluster false-link probability above ~1/n makes transitive closure produce giant components — though its formal ER treatment is a gap.

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

The testable corollary of Johndrow-Lum-Dunson: demographic-ER scaling curves flatten to a floor predicted by field collision mass, regardless of model capacity. Reimers & Gurevych (ACL 2021): dense-retrieval false positives grow faster with index size than BM25's and rankings can invert — one study, IR metrics, not entity-level ER. FRVT-derived functional forms (FPIR ~linear, FNIR ~log in gallery size) are biometric-only, and hubness may make embedding FP growth super-linear for "wolf" records. UIDAI's accuracy figures (~0.057% FPIR / ~0.035% FNIR from the 2012 PoC; later >99.8% claims) are unreplicated agency self-reports — order-of-magnitude anchors at best. Uniqueness-of-demographics numbers are estimator-dependent: Sweeney's 87% on {ZIP5, sex, DOB} vs Golle's ~63% vs Rocher et al.'s copula-extrapolated 99.98% with 15 attributes — evidence that uniqueness numbers do not transfer across estimators. PVS false-match rates in the low single-digit percent range (CARRA WP 2014-02) are single-agency clerical estimates. Meta-recognition/EVM per-record Weibull/GPD calibration (Scheirer et al. 2011; Rudd et al. 2018) is an untested transplant to record embeddings. Cohort-varying first-name frequencies almost certainly push name×DOB collision mass above the independence product — magnitude unmeasured but computable from public SSA/census tables. Microclustering prior misspecification (Zanella et al. 2016) has untested practical effect at scale; Splink's ~1e8 capability is one organization's claim. The percolation prediction — discontinuous entity-precision collapse when false-link rate crosses ~c/n — is theory awaiting ER-specific measurement.

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

Corpus-size sweeps exist in biometrics (FRVT to ~1e7; MegaFace to 1e6 distractors) and IR (index-size sweeps; BigANN 1e9 infrastructure) — but not for demographic person ER with entity-level metrics. Tail-model extrapolation is explored in biometrics (Daugman's validated binomial tail; Weibull/GPD meta-recognition), not for learned whole-record embeddings. Population-dependent FS machinery, collision/isonymy/surprisal accounting (as separate pieces), impossibility theory, imbalance-aware sampling evaluation, blocking scalability countermeasures, operational 1e8–1e9 architectures, synthetic generators at ~1e5–1e6 (GeCo/Febrl), and learning-curve fitting methodology (Viering & Loog 2022) are all explored individually. Re-showing that precision decays with n, or that galleries degrade identification, would be re-confirmation; the unassembled combinations are the gaps below.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

No published work fits error-vs-n curves on nested subsamples of a person corpus and validates extrapolation out-of-sample — the lab's most direct opening, SCL-01. No EVT analysis of learned whole-record non-match tails exists (tail index, per-record GPD fits, predicted-vs-observed FP counts, union-bound validity under hubness) — SCL-02. No assembled irreducible-ambiguity budget (joint name×DOB×address collision mass with cohort correlations, twins, households) despite public inputs — SCL-04, with MET-06. ## 6. Billion-scale ANN and industrial ER systems

### What is settled

At 1e8–1e9 vectors, exact online kNN is infeasible and full-precision in-RAM storage usually uneconomical; the field universally combines ANN indexes with compression or SSD/GPU tiering, and FAISS guidance calls compression "mandatory" at 10M–1B vectors per server (FAISS wiki, per sweep sources). The recall/latency/memory/build-time tradeoff is genuinely multi-objective — no index family dominates; the operating constraint dictates the choice — reproduced across every independent benchmark (per sweep sources). The settled methodological norm is iso-recall comparison: throughput is only comparable at matched recall — enforced by both benchmark suites, aligned with this lab's MET-02 discipline (Simhadri et al., per sweep sources).

Graph indexes (HNSW, NSG, Vamana/DiskANN) are the strong in-memory baselines at 1M–100M, HNSW the universal reference; IVF plus PQ/OPQ residual encoding is the decades-tested billion-scale compressed in-RAM workhorse (FAISS wiki; VIBE, per sweep sources). GPU acceleration gives order-of-magnitude speedups for build and brute-force/IVF search — direction widely replicated, multipliers workload-specific (RAPIDS cuVS, per sweep sources). Streaming ANN is a distinct, harder regime requiring dedicated designs (DiskANN "fresh," SPFresh, CAGRA updates); big-ann-benchmarks added a separate streaming track for this reason.

On the ER side, blocking is the settled first stage — all-pairs is O(n^2); production systems cut comparisons to a tiny fraction (Zingg reports 0.05–1% typical; vendor-reported). Fellegi–Sunter probabilistic linkage is the settled statistical foundation of deployed person ER, with Splink an explicit implementation at national statistics offices. Critically: deterministic/rule and lexical BM25 blocking remain competitive at hundreds-of-millions scale (Splink block_on; Sparkly), and learned-embedding blocking has NOT displaced them as the default (all per sweep sources).

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

Every headline number below is single-benchmark or author-reported, on image/text/web embeddings — never person records. big-ann'21 T2 (SSD, 8 vCPU/64GB): DiskANN baseline recall@10 ~0.90–0.95 on bigann/deep/msspacev/msturing-1B but only ~0.163 (ssnpp range) and ~0.489 (text2image OOD) — test whether OOD-style query/index shift similarly collapses recall on person embeddings. T1 (in-memory, 32 vCPU/64GB, 10k QPS): FAISS baseline recall@10 ~0.06–0.75, winners +~5–13 points — the testable in-RAM ceiling (Simhadri et al., per sweep sources). DiskANN's NeurIPS'19 claim of ~95% recall@1 at few-ms latency for 1B points on one 64GB+SSD node is authors' framing, corroborated in direction by the T2 baseline. T3 numbers (NVIDIA multi-GPU ~2.96M QPS aggregate; $429,635–$1,298,437 per 100K QPS capex+5yr power; 0.095–0.204 W-s/query) come from cost/power boards labeled unofficial pending anomaly resolution — the only published billion-scale ANN cost model: a starting point, not ground truth.

big-ann'23 winners (10M scale) hugely beat baselines at >=90% recall@10 (parlayivf ~30,744 QPS filtered; pyanns ~22,476 QPS OOD, ~6,281 QPS sparse), but the pyanns OOD entry carried an undisclosed organizer (Zilliz) conflict — needs independent replication (per sweep sources). SPANN's recall-at-equal-memory advantage over DiskANN, SPFresh's stable-recall-under-updates claim, and ScaNN's million-scale SOTA are authors' framing, unconfirmed. ## 7. Training pressures for record embeddings

*Sweep caveat: its search budget was exhausted, egress blocked (arxiv, semanticscholar, huggingface, zenodo); findings rest on knowledge through January 2026 plus PyPI inspection this session (marked VERIFIED). Read "no work exists" as "none known as of early 2026."*

### What is settled

InfoNCE-style contrastive training with in-batch negatives is the standard recipe behind SimCLR, DPR, SBERT, Contriever, E5, BGE, and GTE; sentence-transformers 6.0.0 documents its MultipleNegativesRankingLoss as InfoNCE at default scale 20, temperature 0.05 (VERIFIED). Mined hard negatives reliably beat random or in-batch-only negatives (replicated: DPR, ANCE, RocketQA, NV-Retriever, arctic-embed) and are productized (VERIFIED); false negatives contaminating them are a documented failure mode whose standard mitigations — cross-encoder rescoring, margins relative to the positive, rank-range skipping, guide-model filtering — all ship in sentence-transformers 6.0.0 (VERIFIED). The two-stage recipe (weakly supervised contrastive pre-training, then hard-negative fine-tuning) was independently converged on by E5, BGE, and GTE. Larger negative pools help with diminishing returns; the GradCache-style memory workarounds are standard (VERIFIED).

Fine-tuning transformers over Ditto-serialized records decisively beat the word-embedding deep-EM generation (replicated since 2020). Subword embeddings degrade substantially under character noise; character/byte modeling or typo-augmented training restores much robustness (replicated: CharacterBERT, ByT5, CANINE, two independent dense-retrieval typo lines). Classic string/phonetic name measures (Jaro-Winkler, q-grams, Double-Metaphone) are strong, hard-to-beat baselines (repeated since Christen 2006). Alignment/uniformity is an established diagnostic — an analysis tool, not a downstream-quality predictor. Matryoshka training is production-standard across providers; truncation-with-modest-loss is deployment-replicated, retention numbers per-model. Billion-scale ANN machinery is mature for in-distribution image/text workloads; the framing "search is solved, only embedding quality is open" is unproven for noisy person embeddings (2021-era 1e9 leaderboards, OOD collapse — §6) and is itself under test in BAS-02/SCL-02. Neural scores are miscalibrated by default; post-hoc calibration is established (Guo 2017 and replications). Fellegi–Sunter with EM-estimated parameters is the calibrated production baseline (Splink 4 ships the recipe — VERIFIED via PyPI inspection; the 100M+ deployment scale is a maintainer claim, measured in BAS-01). Two independent 2020 studies (Musgrave; Roth) showed budget-matched comparisons collapse most claimed loss differences — expect loss effects smaller than sampling/augmentation effects. Pairwise F1 is a flawed headline metric (Hand & Christen 2018), entity-level evaluation is the target, and the Magellan suite's pair-level random splits share entities across train/test (a factual construction property).

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

Augmentation and supervision: Ditto's ~9-point stack with augmentation worth 1–3 points in ablation (single-system); Sudowoodo's near-supervised quality with few labels (authors' benchmarks); R-SupCon's SupCon-beats-cross-encoder claim in low-label regimes (WDC-centric, untested on PII-like records); Rotom's meta-learned augmentation win (single group).

Mining geometry: NV-Retriever's TopK-PercPos(95%) as best configuration (vendor claim on MTEB-style tasks; VERIFIED only as the recommended sentence-transformers default); SimANS's ambiguity-band sampling (single group); the FaceNet/Xuan warning that hardest negatives degrade training — critical where they are family members or true duplicates; debiased/hard-aware losses (untested at ER scale); GISTEmbed's guide filtering (single author).

Tokenization and robustness: CharacterBERT-DR's typo augmentation plus self-teaching consistency loss recovering most typo-lost effectiveness at near-zero clean cost — the sweep's best pressure->invariance->metric chain; replicate on person records; ByT5/CANINE's large noise gains at 2–8x inference cost (passage-level; short-field PII transfer untested); CIKM 2022's brittleness catalog (replicate for bi-encoders); TabLLM's templates-beat-JSON (LLM classifiers, not embeddings).

Compression and diagnostics: MRL's near-full retention at ~14x dimension reduction (single-paper; blocking recall unmeasured); int8/binary quantization keeping ~95–99% quality at 4–32x memory savings (vendor numbers, nDCG-style metrics); low temperature as a hardness-weighting knob interacting with false-negative rate (mechanism believed, unquantified); alignment/uniformity and RankMe as ER-quality predictors (unvalidated); anisotropy's causal role (contested — probe, not target). Elsewhere: entity leakage inflating benchmark scores (quantify for embeddings); embedding blocking winning only on textual/dirty data (product-centric, single-group); learned name encoders beating edit distance (small-scale — does one beat Jaro-Winkler inside a full-record system?); Splink's ~1M records/minute laptop claim (VERIFIED as a claim, worth independent measurement).

## 8. Supervision provenance and label circularity

Sweep caveat: compiled from model knowledge (cutoff Jan 2026), live search/fetch blocked; URLs, arXiv IDs, and quoted figures unverified; 2026-only work not covered.

### What is settled

Deep networks fit clean structure before memorizing noisy labels — the replicated early-learning/small-loss phenomenon (Zhang 2017; Arpit 2017, per sweep sources) behind co-teaching, DivideMix, ELR. Label models over noisy labeling functions train useful end models without gold labels, validated in Google-scale deployment (Snorkel, DryBell, WRENCH, per sweep sources). Fellegi-Sunter m/u parameters are estimable without labels via EM (Winkler; Census PVS and UK Splink practice, per sweep sources) — a teacher FS system itself needs no gold labels.

Administrative identifiers carry duplicates and overlays at materially nonzero, site-varying rates (AHIMA, ONC 2014, Pew 2018, per sweep sources) — existence settled, no specific rate is. Linkage error is differential across demographic subgroups (Harron/Hagger-Johnson HES line, per sweep sources), so label noise inherited from admin keys is instance- and subgroup-dependent, not class-conditional. Hard-negative mining against incompletely labeled corpora yields false-negative contamination; filtering mined negatives with a stronger model helps (RocketQA; standard in E5/BGE/GTE, per sweep sources) — phenomenon and remedy settled, percentages dataset-specific. Sparse labels distort training and evaluation rankings (MS MARCO "holes", per sweep sources).

A student is not capped at teacher quality — students surpass teachers under noise, augmentation, ensembling, and abstention (Noisy Student, born-again networks, weak-to-strong generalization, per sweep sources) — but agreement metrics against teacher-correlated labels remain biased toward the teacher (imperfect-gold-standard bias; Doidge & Harron; GUILD, per sweep sources). Pairwise errors amplify at the entity level: sampling positives within noisy clusters turns one wrong link into O(k^2) wrong pairs (arithmetic; Christen 2012, per sweep sources). Admin-key supervision is positive-unlabeled — key-sharing pairs are mostly reliable positives, non-sharing pairs unlabeled, not negative (Elkan & Noto 2008, per sweep sources; rarely invoked in ER). Corruption-positive self-supervision suffices for blocking (DeepBlocker, Sudowoodo, per sweep sources) — settled only at benchmark scale and only for blocking.

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

- MPI duplicate rates ~8–12% typical, ~20%+ poorly governed, <1% well governed; ~18% within-org, ~50% cross-org failure (AHIMA/ONC; Black Book 2018; Pew 2018) — survey/vendor provenance; verify before trusting any EnterpriseID.
- ~70% of mined hard negatives in MS MARCO were actually positives (RocketQA, single audit); ~3.3% average test-set label error across 10 benchmarks (Northcutt et al.) — imply duplication-scaled FN contamination and auditable EM benchmark errors; untested for person ER.
- "NCID is reliable ground truth" — untested assumption inherited by dozens of papers (fastLink/APSR 2019; Christen-group benchmarks); testable via capture-recapture, Belin-Rubin calibration, or clerical audit.
- Admin-linkage missed matches reach ~5–10% in some subgroups, false matches well under 1% (Harron HES line) — a testable prior that admin-key noise is FN-dominated and subgroup-skewed.
- Historical automated linking carries ~15–30% false positives by method (Bailey vs Abramitzky groups, contested); genealogy supervision yields ~2–3x more links at comparable precision (Census Tree, single group), selection-biased — truth-source choice drives conclusions.
- Benchmark no-label claims (ZeroER, DeepBlocker/Sudowoodo, LLM matching, GPL distillation) may not transfer to noisy person records.
- Noise-robust contrastive objectives (RINCE, Sel-CL, MoPro) tolerate positive-pair noise (vision evidence, untested on record pairs); small-loss selection and confident learning assume class-conditional noise, violated by ER's instance-dependent noise. Label-model aggregation beats any single linker only under assumed independence — ER linkers share name/DOB features.
- Teacher-high-confidence-only training degrades hard-pair performance (confirmation bias); an embedding student can exceed an FS teacher on independently adjudicated disagreements (weak-to-strong extrapolation) — the lab's core circularity question; requires a teacher-independent eval set.
- GeCo-style corruption realism rests on ~2009-era parameters — transfer gap unquantified. WDC's identifier-derived labels carry unpublished residual error. Swedish personal identity numbers change over ~1% of lifetimes (Ludvigsson 2009, single source) — even best-case keys imply ~1e6 mislabels per 1% error at 1e8 scale (arithmetic).

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

Positive-pair generation has been explored via synthetic corruption (DeepBlocker, Sudowoodo, MixDA, Rotom), identifier-distant supervision (WDC GTIN/MPN, NCVR NCID, PVS/PIK), genealogy links (Census Tree), and legacy-linker distillation (industry EMPI pipelines; Census MAMBA), the last mostly undocumented as methodology. Low/no-label EM regimes exist on benchmarks (ZeroER, CollaborEM, PromptEM, active learning, LLM distillation). The LNL toolbox (co-teaching, DivideMix, ELR, cleanlab) and FN-aware negative pipelines (ANCE, RocketQA, SimANS, consistency filtering) are mature — but in vision and single-positive retrieval, not ER. Contrastive matchers (Ditto, R-SupCon, SC-Block, LinkTransformer) take label provenance as given, and statistical linkage-error estimation without gold labels (Belin-Rubin, Winkler, capture-recapture, GUILD) has never been wired into training-label pipelines. Re-demonstrating any of these at home is re-confirmation; the novelty is the splice — seeded realistic (FN-dominated, subgroup-skewed, cluster-structured) label noise into embedding-ER training with entity-level, circularity-aware evaluation.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

The LNL and ER literatures are disjoint as of early 2026: a seeded-noise LNL-method sweep scored on entity-level metrics is novel — TRN-01 and TRN-02 (FN contamination in dedup-dense negative mining — the vicious cycle where a teacher's missed duplicate becomes a training hard negative). No error model of administrative keys as training labels exists — MET-05 (NCID error audit, feasibility gate) with MET-06, the teacher-error sensitivity curve feeding MET-04. Circular evaluation (embedding vs FS when the baseline generated the labels) has no standard protocol — MET-05, MET-07, MET-02/MET-03, against BAS-01's FS teacher/baseline. Synthetic-to-real corruption gap — NSE-01, NSE-02, NSE-03. Fairness circuit (subgroup-skewed label noise inherited by embeddings) — FAIR-01 under NSE-01's measured exposure. Cluster-aware noise handling and transitivity-violation noise signals — CLU-01 with TRN-02, which also touches, without owning, teacher-confident selection bias. Unmapped: dependence-aware label-model aggregation at 1e10+ pairs; LLM annotation of person-PII pairs; a public person-scale benchmark with key-independent gold labels — MET-05 is a feasibility probe toward the last, not a commitment.

## 9. Demographic disparities in person matching

### What is settled

Linkage error is not randomly distributed. Missed-match and false-match rates run systematically higher for ethnic-minority, immigrant, and socially disadvantaged records, reproduced by independent groups across countries and systems — UK Hospital Episode Statistics (Hagger-Johnson et al.), US National Death Index linkage (Lariscy/NCHS), US Census PVS/PIK, the NZ IDI, and Australian Indigenous health data (all per sweep sources). The existence and direction of the effect is safe to build on; magnitudes are dataset-specific. The dominant mechanism is pre-algorithmic: minority, immigrant, mobile, and low-income records more often carry missing or inconsistent identifiers (missing SSN, placeholder dates of birth, unstable addresses), so unequal identifier quality alone produces unequal linkage regardless of algorithm.

Further reality-tested findings: data capture built around the Anglo single-given + single-family name model degrades matching for populations that do not fit it — Hispanic dual surnames and particles, marital surname change, East-Asian name order and romanization variants, Arabic transliteration — a repeatedly documented failure mode. Exact-match and name-only matching causes disparate harm at deployment scale, because common-surname mass concentrates in minority populations: Florida's 2000 felon purge, Interstate Crosscheck (Goel et al., per sweep sources), Georgia's "exact match" holds, and TransUnion's OFAC name-only screening (TransUnion v. Ramirez, per sweep sources) share one over-flagging pattern. Classical phonetic encodings (Soundex and relatives) are Anglo-centric and lose accuracy on non-Anglo names — the settled motivation for NYSIIS, the Metaphones, Daitch-Mokotoff, and Beider-Morse. Conversely, relaxing exact-name agreement and population-specific name handling (Hispanic/Asian name algorithms, nickname tables, romanization standardization) measurably improve minority linkage (Lancet Digital Health editorial; NCHS practice, per sweep sources) — directionally settled.

The measurement toolkit is settled: gold-standard-subset error quantification, linked-vs-unlinked comparison, and threshold sensitivity, with GUILD and the Gilbert/Harron guide as standards and disaggregated-by-subgroup reporting the default. Census PIK assignment is non-random (lower for children, minorities, immigrants, movers, low-income persons); 2010 PVS changes raised the person-weighted validated share ~88.1% to ~92.6% (official, system-specific, per sweep sources), attenuating not eliminating the deficits; reweighting is the accepted mitigation.

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

- Field missingness plus low "informational diversity" in race/ethnicity strata drive probabilistic-matching accuracy declines (>0.8 overall) — JMIR Formative Research 2026, single exploratory study, one algorithm family.
- Cluster-based name embeddings cut missed-match disparity vs TF-adjusted string similarity (+16.3% Indigenous/PI, +10.2% Hispanic, +3.8% Asian) but raise false matches in high-corruption groups; unequal exposure to identifier error dominates mechanism heterogeneity (~1% NH White forenames corrupted vs >1/3 forenames, >2/3 surnames Indigenous/PI; arXiv 2601.07693) — single preprint, simulated corruption, one registry.
- Referential matching F=0.966 vs probabilistic F~0.637 (Grannis et al. JAMIA 2022) — single study, single HIE, no subgroup breakdown; the 0.637 figure carries a sweep-internal citation conflict (reported as sensitivity in §1's area, F-score here — verify against the paper).
- EM unfairness arises under group over-representation and higher within-group name similarity; PPV-parity/TPR-parity most revealing under class imbalance (Shahbazi et al. VLDB 2023; FairEM360) — one group's suite, two constructed datasets.
- Deep matchers can look fair at a threshold yet carry threshold-independent score bias, removable by post-processing (arXiv 2405.20051 / 2411.01685) — single lineage, unreplicated.
- Race-linked bias appears across Soundex/NYSIIS/Metaphone but gender bias does not (PPRL phonetic-bias study, 2023) — single study; do not generalize the null.
- Name-ethnicity classifiers carry large uneven accuracy biases ("Equal accuracy for Andrew and Abubakar") — single audit; these classifiers supply both matching features and the labels used to measure disparity.
- NIST FRVT Part 3's 10x–100x demographic false-positive differentials are adjacent modality (face biometrics): direction robust, magnitudes vendor-specific, not transferable to record ER.
- SAVE citizenship-matching error figures (>50% Boone County MO; >=14% Denton County TX among flagged voters) — journalistic single-jurisdiction points, a harm signal, not controlled estimates.
- NZ IDI: 97.3% Maori-descent vs 98.4% non-Maori linkage (1.1pp) — one official statistic.
- Randall et al. 2018: worst linkage for younger and remote-area persons, with some sociodemographic associations reversing direction across datasets — "who is disadvantaged" is partly an artifact of data capture.

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

Disparity has already been measured across matcher families (deterministic, Fellegi-Sunter, referential, classical ML, name/word and cluster-based name embeddings, transformer linkage, LLM matchers, hybrid contrastive+rules pipelines) and name-handling techniques (phonetic codes, nickname tables, NDI Hispanic/Asian algorithms, romanization/transliteration standardization). Proposed interventions include fairness-aware data preparation, threshold-independent score-bias metrics, score calibration, IPW/entropy-balancing reweighting, and relaxing exact-name agreement. Evaluation infrastructure exists: GUILD, the Gilbert/Harron trio, FairEM360/fair_entity_matching suites, manually reviewed gold standards, simulated-corruption benchmarking. Populations covered span patient matching, NDI, historical census, PVS/PIK, voter rolls, UK HES, NZ IDI, Australian Indigenous registries, refugee cohorts, and PPRL. Re-showing that disparity exists, that name-only matching over-flags minorities, or that Soundex is Anglo-centric is re-confirmation. The novelty bar sits at joint two-sided, entity-level, uncertainty-quantified disparity comparison under measured (not assumed) exposure.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

Entity-level disparity at scale is absent — how pair-level error disparity aggregates into subgroup over-merging vs fragmentation is open: FAIR-01 with CLU-01 and MET-03 (entity-level bootstrap units), extrapolated via SCL-01. Joint reporting of both error types by subgroup is rare — the one preprint on the trade-off shows equity gains on one side paid for on the other: FAIR-01 directly. Subpopulation-specific noise models are underdeveloped while exposure appears to dominate: NSE-01 (measured prevalence and group exposure), NSE-02, NSE-03. Causal decomposition into exposure vs mechanism exists in ~one unreplicated preprint: FAIR-01 with NSE-01, via the lab's mediation machinery (TRN-03 pattern). Rigorous head-to-head disparity comparison (CIs, paired tests) is scarce: MET-02, MET-03, MET-04. No consensus fairness metric under ER's class imbalance: MET-01, MET-02. English-centric nickname resources: TRN-06. Non-Latin-script matching validated only on small corpora: touched by TRN-04 and PRS-01; population-scale validation unmapped. Noisy demographic measurement labels (circular disparity estimates), intersectional disparities, and multi-site replication of a matcher's disparity signature remain honest residual gaps, beyond sensitivity analyses FAIR-01 can carry.

## 10. Privacy, leakage, and governance of person embeddings

**Provenance caveat (from the sweep, covering this entire section):** live retrieval was impossible — search budget exhausted, fetch egress policy-blocked (403) for arXiv, publisher, and state-election domains — so this section is synthesized from model knowledge (Jan-2026 cutoff), citations recalled, not re-verified. Verify every URL, number, and year, and check for 2026 updates (CJEU C-413/23 P, EDPB pseudonymization guidance, newer inversion/MIA work) before citing externally.

### What is settled

Privacy-preserving record linkage (PPRL) is a ~20-year-mature subfield with this lab's core problem — similarity-preserving PII encodings matched without exposing plaintext — and Bloom-filter q-gram encodings plus the single-vector CLK dominate deployment in health and national-statistics linkage units: "dense encodings of person records" is not a green field (Vatsalan et al. 2013; Schnell et al. 2009, per sweep sources). Unhardened Bloom/CLK encodings are re-identifiable by frequency cryptanalysis against an auxiliary plaintext population — typically a public voter file — replicated by three independent groups (Kuzu 2011; Niedermeyer 2014; Christen 2017, per sweep sources). Graph-matching attacks go further, re-identifying records across schemes from similarity structure alone (Vidanage et al. 2020, per sweep sources): preserving pairwise similarity necessarily leaks re-identifiable relational structure — the lesson transferring most directly to learned ER embeddings. Hardening (salting, balanced filters, XOR-folding, BLIP/DP noise) reduces but never eliminates attack surface, at a linkage-quality cost; per-record salting defeats frequency attacks but breaks cross-party linkability, so deployments rarely use it (Christen et al. 2020, per sweep sources).

Text embeddings leak their inputs — inversion, attribute inference, and membership signal, demonstrated across independent groups (Song & Raghunathan CCS'20; Pan et al. S&P'20; Morris et al. EMNLP'23, per sweep sources) — so released dense vectors are not an anonymous release of the text; recovery rates stay hypotheses (below). Models memorize training data, sharply more for duplicated/outlier examples (Carlini et al. 2021/2023, per sweep sources): a released checkpoint is itself a leakage vector, making deduplication of the 1e8 corpus a first-order privacy control. Under GDPR, pseudonymized data remains personal data, identifiability turns on "means reasonably likely to be used" (Recital 26), and only irreversible anonymization exits scope — released vectors, indexes, and possibly weights are presumptively regulated absent a defended anonymity argument. US voter files carry heterogeneous state permissible-use statutes (non-commercial, no-resale, no-internet-posting) that a freely redistributable 1e8 real-data benchmark would violate; HIPAA Safe Harbor strips exactly the fields a person-ER benchmark needs, taking real health corpora off the table (HHS; NCSL, per sweep sources).

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

vec2text reports ~92% exact reconstruction for ~32-token inputs, degrading with length (Morris et al. 2023, per sweep sources; number recalled, unverified) — a single-lineage result on general sentence embeddings. The decision-relevant open question for anyone extending this work toward deployment — not pursued here, per the §0 scope note — is whether a vec2text-class inverter recovers fields from ER-tuned whole-record embeddings: low-entropy templated PII may make it easier, metric-learning collapse harder. Claims that inversion transfers across encoders and partially survives noise defenses (Morris et al. ICLR'24 and follow-ons, per sweep sources) are recent, not independently replicated. Vidanage's graph-matching re-identification rates are one group's numbers, not independently replicated; anyone extending this work toward releasing an index would need to re-run them against that index — an experiment this lab explicitly does not perform (§0 scope note). Shokri-lineage MIA tracks overfitting/duplication/outlier status, implying attacks on a deduplicated 1e8 model are weak on average, non-trivial for tail records — and "weak on average" does not discharge per-record legal risk. Two contested regulatory positions remain hypotheses: the Hamburg DPA's July-2024 view that weights alone are not personal data (conflicts with memorization evidence and EDPB Opinion 28/2024 — do not rely on it), and the EDPB opinion as binding direction: any release requires an affirmative anonymity argument or DPIA plus lawful basis. Also unproven either way: contrastive objectives reducing literal invertibility while increasing linkage/attribute leakage; 2019–2025 neural PPRL encodings improving the privacy/utility frontier (authors' framing, untested against red teams); SLK-581-style deterministic keys being re-identifiable and collision-prone rather than governance-safe.

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

Encoding families (phonetic/statistical keys, Bloom/CLK, tabulation MinHash, two-step hash, LSH sketches, SMC/HE linkage, neural PPRL), hardening axes, and the attack taxonomy (CSP, frequency/atom, pattern-mining, structure-only graph matching) are all mapped, plus embedding leakage on text encoders and memorization on generative LMs. The legal axis (GDPR identifiability, SRB v EDPS, EDPB 28/2024, HIPAA, state voter statutes, Common Rule) is charted; synthetic-PII generation (Febrl corruptor, GeCo) is the established redistribution-safe substitute. Re-demonstrating "embeddings leak" on generic encoders, or another unaudited neural PPRL encoding, would be re-confirmation. Novelty must be argued on scale (1e8–1e9), the non-adversarial ER setting, threat model, and entity-level evaluation — not on dense PII encoding being new. No prior work covers leakage of metric-learning-trained whole-record person embeddings, or any openly redistributable real corpus above NCVR's single-digit-millions ceiling.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

**This lab deliberately does not engage these gaps** (see the scope note in §0): privacy constraints are out of scope by design, so no experiment in the program probes leakage, inversion, membership inference, DP training, or the legal status of embedding artifacts. The gaps are nonetheless real and documented here to mark the boundary honestly: there is no standard threat model, attack suite, or leakage metric for learned whole-record person embeddings (the object of study falls between the inversion/MIA literature, which targets generic sentence embeddings, and the PPRL attack literature, which targets Bloom filters); whether ER metric-learning makes embeddings more or less leaky than reconstruction objectives is open; the legal status of an index or checkpoint under GDPR/state law is undecided; DP-SGD utility for contrastive ER at 1e8 is unmeasured; and the release-artifact hierarchy (vectors vs index vs checkpoint vs synthetic+code) is unsettled. Anyone extending this lab toward deployment on non-public data should treat this section as the reading list for the work we chose not to do. Two gaps from this area do map into the lab, because they are noise-model gaps rather than privacy experiments: the absent governance-clean large-scale benchmark and the unvalidated fidelity of Febrl/GeCo-style synthetic noise feed NSE-01/NSE-02, with NSE-03 checking ranking invariance across noise models. The lab's only privacy-adjacent behavior is passive hygiene, not research: public-record and synthetic data only, download scripts rather than data, licensing terms recorded in DATA_GOVERNANCE.md.

## 11. Upstream standardization and parsing

### What is settled

Production Fellegi-Sunter pipelines presuppose heavy upstream standardization: Census PVS standardizes names/addresses and blocks on standardized geography (Wagner & Layne 2014, per sweep sources); IPUMS hlink ships config-driven preprocessing ahead of scoring (verified); splink's docs teach pre-matching derived columns (postcode-to-lat/long, Double Metaphone, full-name concatenation for TF; verified); USPS CASS institutionalizes address standardization — multi-deployment, multi-decade practice. Parsing tooling splits into sequence labelers (libpostal, trained on >1B OSM/OpenAddresses addresses; usaddress/probablepeople CRFs; Febrl-era HMMs) and neural seq2seq parsers (deepparse); the CRF tools disclaim accuracy on arbitrary input (verified). Published accuracy is in-distribution (libpostal's 99.45% full-parse on held-out data from its own training distribution; deepparse's ~99% on clean data from its 20 trained countries); learned parsers degrade badly OOD (deepparse README: ~36% zero-shot Japan to ~96% Paraguay) — the qualitative fact is settled, the magnitudes per-source.

Central and equally settled: every widely used noise generator emits fielded records and corrupts per field (verified — Febrl dsgen and GeCo schemas, Gecko DataFrames, pseudopeople schema/noise files; no unparsed-concatenation or format-regime channel): open generators cannot express pre-standardization raw forms or parsing error as noise. Schema-agnostic ER over concatenated text is an established lineage (JedAI, verified; DeepBlocker; Sparkly; Ditto serialization) — existence settled, superiority not. Deep-ER benchmarks are born fielded, "Dirty" variants synthesized by relocating attribute values; none provides genuinely raw person strings with ground-truth parses plus match labels. TF adjustment computes from observed value frequencies; unstandardized variants fragment a value's frequency mass; splink's TF docs (verified) state no standardization prerequisites — mechanism settled, sensitivity unmeasured. Replicated, fairness-relevant: differential linkage error by ethnic/cultural group, name handling implicated (Hagger-Johnson et al.; Harron et al.); geocoding quality depends on standardization, worse for rural/PO-box/non-standard addresses (Cayo & Talbot 2003; Zandbergen; Oliver et al. 2005). The "data preparation matters as much as the matcher" orthodoxy (Herzog, Scheuren & Winkler 2007; Christen 2012) rests on thinner evidence than its citation count — largely Census experience — with a published counterexample (Randall et al. 2013).

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

Herzog/Scheuren/Winkler claim large match losses absent parsing; Randall et al. (2013) found negligible-to-negative cleaning effects on already administratively standardized hospital data (not re-verified; publisher blocked) — testable synthesis: marginal value of standardization ≈ f(input rawness); Randall's null may extend to TF and blocking on pre-standardized data yet flip to first-order on raw data (neither half measured). Grannis et al. (JAMIA 2019), standardization improving patient matching: single-group, policy-entangled (ONC Project US@); not re-fetched. libpostal's 99.45% likely does not transfer to operational addresses — its README cites a Senzing retrain +4.3% on a curated 12,950-address set. Deepparse's 36–96% zero-shot spread suggests learned-parser error concentrates in the same multicultural/OOD segments as person-ER error — amplification hypothesis. Churches et al. (2002), HMM beating rules (AutoStan) on addresses: early, single-group (not re-verified). Deep matchers degrading less on "Dirty" data (DeepMatcher, Ditto) is replicated almost solely on one benchmark family — correlated replication. Ditto's ablations: span typing/normalization improves PLM F1 — even embedding matchers benefit from partial standardization, undercutting the strong "preprocessing unnecessary" claim. Abramitzky et al. (2021): NYSIIS-style choices trade recall for precision and change downstream conclusions (magnitudes not re-verified). ONC organizers' framing — top performers won via preprocessing and tuning — is author-framed, single-event. splink's concatenated-full-name-TF-beats-per-field-TF claim is doc-only. Comber & Arribas-Bel (2019), embedding address matching competitive with CRF matching: single study (not re-verified). The lab's confound thesis, testably stated: a substantial fraction (plausibly the majority on Dirty-style data) of the embedding-over-rules margin disappears once the rules baseline gets equivalent standardization — untested in either direction.

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

Structure corruption exists as a benchmark axis (DeepMatcher's Structured/Textual/Dirty) but is synthetic and narrow; PLM serialization choices are explored in the Ditto lineage (Brunner & Stockinger 2020), only downstream of clean fields; skip-parsing blocking is well explored (token/meta-blocking, DeepBlocker, Sparkly). The parser axis is fully traversed: rules/lexicons (CASS, AutoStan) → HMM (Churches 2002; Febrl) → CRF (usaddress, probablepeople, libpostal) → seq2seq (deepparse) → LLM cleaners (Jellyfish 2024; Peeters & Bizer). Cleaning ablations exist in FS-only pipelines (Randall; Grannis — no embedding arm); preprocessing-by-algorithm comparisons in census linking (Abramitzky et al.; hlink); CleanML (ICDE 2021) supplies the factorial methodology, unapplied to ER. Field-level corruption, phonetic keys, and TF mechanics are mature. Another Dirty-robustness demo or in-distribution parser bake-off is re-confirmation; nothing varies standardization as a controlled factor across embedding and rules arms.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

No published study treats standardization/parsing as a controlled factor in embeddings-vs-rules comparisons; a {raw, lightly standardized, fully standardized} × {FS+rules, embedding} factorial with entity-level metrics and paired tests would be novel — PRS-01 runs the 2×2 slice of this design ({raw, fully standardized} × {FS+rules, embedding}), with BAS-01's tuned FS arm and MET-02/MET-03's protocol. No open generator emits pre-standardization raw forms with ground-truth parses; an invertible "de-standardizer" with ground-truth spans feeds PRS-01, its realism checked by NSE-02. Parser error on operational person data is essentially unpublished — PRS-01's measurement layer. TF sensitivity to standardization is unmeasured yet cheap — PRS-01 with BAS-01. Blocking-key recall vs parsing error is unstudied despite being the dominant 1e8–1e9 bottleneck — BAS-02 at matched budget. Entity-level evaluation of preprocessing effects does not exist (all cited studies are pairwise) — PRS-01 via CLU-01/MET-03. Nothing decomposes the learned-matcher margin into preprocessing absorption vs better similarity vs implicit TF effects — the lab's mediation methodology (TRN-03) on the PRS-01 factorial, TRN-05 on the serialization slice. Whether learned parsers amplify the replicated ethnic linkage-error gap is untested — FAIR-01. Unmapped: geocode-as-linkage-key error propagation and normalization-leakage audits of gold standards (MET-07 covers split optimism, a different channel). Sweep completeness caveat, carried: web-search budget exhausted, egress limited to GitHub — before claiming PRS-01 novelty, search post-2023 work on data-preparation impact on entity matching, LLM address normalization, and pseudopeople extensions.

## 12. Datasets, benchmarks, and noise generators

### What is settled

Privacy constraints have made "synthetic or synthetically corrupted data with known ground truth" the dominant strategy for shareable person-ER benchmarks; no public benchmark of real labeled person records exists at large scale. The pattern holds across Febrl, GeCO, BPID, ONC, pseudopeople, Leipzig NCVoter, and the splink datasets (per sweep sources). The one real-data workhorse is the NC voter registration corpus: freely downloadable public records with a statewide person identifier (NCID) and 20+ years of point-in-time snapshots, used as a benchmark substrate by multiple independent groups — Christen/ANU temporal linkage, Leipzig FAMER, HPI's repeatability dataset, political-science linkage such as fastLink — the de facto real-data standard (NCSBE sources, per sweep). Febrl (last release Dec 2011, Python 2) remains a de facto standard through independent redistribution (recordlinkage v0.16, splink_datasets), and the Febrl-dsgen/GeCO corruption taxonomy — keyboard/typo, OCR, phonetic, character edits, missingness, value swaps — is the reference error model reused by Gecko and pseudopeople (Tran et al. 2013; Jugl et al. 2024; pseudopeople docs). US state voter files are the only freely-or-cheaply accessible real-person corpora at 1e6–1e7 scale, with state-varying access terms (most restrict commercial use; NCSL summary via pablobarbera/voter-files) and fields: NC publishes birth_year/age only; Ohio and Florida publish full DOB. Ground truth in nearly all large real-data evaluations is an administrative key (NCID, SOS_VOTERID, EnterpriseID-style MPI keys), not clerical review — key quality directly bounds evaluation validity.

### Treat as hypotheses, not facts

BPID's claims that its 10K pairs are high-quality annotator labels and that the benchmark is "significantly more challenging" for supervised, deep, and LLM matchers are the vendor-adjacent paper's own framing (single source, AWS team) — test by re-running baselines and auditing label noise. That BPID's synthetic profiles reproduce realistic PII noise is untested independently; compare its error distributions against real NCVR snapshot-to-snapshot diffs before trusting transfer. Whether BPID's 1M panel carries latent entity IDs enabling cluster evaluation is unknown — the paper's framing is pairwise; verify from the Zenodo files (not downloadable from the sandbox). Gecko's claim to reimplement "the most promising aspects" of GeCO with much better performance says nothing about error-distribution fidelity — speed is not realism. pseudopeople's "realistic" noise is IHME's claim, and its own docs concede column noise is independent across columns and of simulant attributes; treat realism as a hypothesis. The Leipzig NCVoter's exact sizes (reported elsewhere as up to 5M records) and corruption parameters need direct confirmation (host blocked), and rankings measured on it may not survive a different corruption model. ONC challenge winners reportedly reached F-measures ~0.96–0.99 on the hidden key — press-reported, unverified, and the public repo has no answer key, so modern reuse must reconstruct truth. NCID's error rate (multiple NCIDs per person after re-registration, transfer across county moves, '#'-masked records) is not systematically quantified in recent literature. Wikidata's ~1e7 "human" (Q5) items as reference corpus needs a current count (host unreachable). The ONC quirks (Excel-serial DOBs, MALE-vs-F coding) may be deliberate noise or publication artifacts — check challenge documentation before normalizing. Ohio's "daily snapshot" portal cadence and retention are unverified.

### Design space already explored (novelty gate)

Rule-based corruption of frequency-table-generated records is thoroughly traversed (Febrl dsgen → GeCO 2013 → TDGen 2012 → Gecko 2024); choosing corruption functions and per-field rates is re-confirmation, not novelty. Microsimulation with configurable administrative observation noise exists at three scales up to 330M simulants (pseudopeople). Real-records-plus-synthetic-corruption benchmarks exist (Leipzig NCVoter, HPI NCVoters, splink historical_50k), as do real longitudinal corpora with administrative-key truth (NC snapshots via NCID; Ohio; Florida) and pairwise-labeled synthetic benchmarks at moderate scale (BPID, ONC). Nickname substitution is built into pseudopeople and the GeCO lineage; document-level OCR degradation exists generically (genalog) but not person-record-specialized. Evaluation so far is overwhelmingly pairwise P/R/F1; cluster metrics appear only in the Leipzig FAMER line, and entity-level evaluation at very large scale is rare — that is where new work lives, not in another corruption toolkit.

### Open gaps and where this lab engages

No public benchmark provides labeled real person records at 1e8–1e9 scale; the assembled route (73 NC snapshots keyed by NCID, ~5–8M rows each, optionally + Ohio + Florida) reaches ~1e8 rows with administrative pseudo-truth, but no curated, quality-audited version has been published — this feeds the lab's scale program (SCL-01; analytical companions SCL-03/SCL-04), gated on MET-05, the NCID error audit — NCID's unquantified split/reuse/masking rates bound everything built on it — with MET-06's identifiability accounting. No independent study calibrates any corruption generator against empirically observed noise; NC snapshot-to-snapshot diffs on stable NCIDs supply a large corpus of genuine name changes, typo fixes, and moves — this is NSE-01 (measured prevalence and group exposure) and NSE-02 (generator fidelity scoring of Febrl/GeCO/Gecko/pseudopeople/BPID against reality). Whether system rankings are invariant across noise models is untested and is exactly NSE-03. Entity-level evaluation at >1e7 records with cluster ground truth is open territory: the lab's MET-02/MET-03 protocol work and CLU-01 clustering evaluation engage it directly. Nickname resources are small, English-only, and provenance-biased — relevant to TRN-06 (nickname supervision vs twin false-merges) and FAIR-01; building a calibrated multilingual resource remains unmapped. Person-record OCR noise calibrated on real scanned forms is unmodeled; the lab does not currently address it (unmapped). Access frictions (pseudopeople gating, BPID Zenodo-only hosting, state-law redistribution limits) are planning constraints, not experiments.
